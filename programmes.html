Programmes

The money grows. So should the child.

A yearly programme keeps families engaged with the Fund. Summer camps teach the habits that make the money count: citizenship, self-discipline and how money works.

Year at a glance

A yearly rhythm every family can expect

Each child and parent hears from the Fund four times a year, in plain language, written for the child's age.

  1. February · New-year goals

    A short message and a family activity sheet: set one saving goal for the year. Schools invited to run a money lesson in assembly.

  2. May · Camp sign-up

    Summer camp places opened to every enrolled child aged 6 and over, with places reserved for children in hardship.

  3. July-August · Summer camps

    One- or two-week day camps across the island, grouped by age. Free for families.

  4. 30 November · Fund Day

    On Republic Day, every family receives the child's annual statement: balance, growth, what it is invested in, and the Fund's results. A public event marks the year.

Channels: portal and app, SMS and WhatsApp, a printed statement for families who want one, and school visits. Families can choose how often they hear from us, but the annual statement always goes out.

Who pays

Programmes are paid for from a separate budget: the Ministry of Education and approved sponsors. Not one dollar comes from children's accounts or the Fund's investments.

Sponsors may support camps but may not market products to children or receive children's data.

Proposed partners

Ministry of Education (schools and venues), uniformed youth groups such as the Barbados Cadet Corps, credit unions (money lessons), the Central Bank of Barbados (financial literacy), the University of the West Indies Cave Hill (Navigator mentoring). All to be confirmed.

Timing: the oldest enrolled children turn 6 in late 2027, so the first Seedlings camp would run in summer 2028. Until then, the programme works through parents.
