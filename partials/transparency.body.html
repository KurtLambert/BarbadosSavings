Governance

Who decides

Government sets the mandate. Independent people choose the managers. Nobody picks individual companies.

Board of Trustees

Approves policy and appoints managers. Majority independent; no sitting politicians. Names published on appointment.

Investment Committee

Five members, at least three independent, including two private-markets specialists. One Ministry of Finance official, who does not vote on manager selection.

Register of interests

Every trustee and committee member declares their business interests each year. No investment in any company they or their families own or direct.

Audit

An external auditor checks accounts and private-asset valuations every year, rotated at least every seven years.

Team and efficiency

Four people. Everything else bought from specialists.

A small core team sets direction and checks the work. Custody, record-keeping, fund management, audit and IT are bought from specialists, so each child's money pays for expertise rather than overhead.

Questions

Common questions

Can the Government take the money back?

No. The money is held in trust for the children. Changing that would need new legislation, debated in Parliament.

Why invest outside Barbados?

Children's future jobs and homes already depend on the Barbados economy. Spreading their savings worldwide protects them if Barbados has a bad decade. Barbados exposure is capped at 20%.

Can the Fund lose money?

In a single year, yes. Over 18 years a diversified fund has historically grown. The Fund becomes more cautious as payouts approach.

When can my child claim?

Between 18 and 25. The exact age and approved uses are still to be confirmed in law.
